# inMyCity Vision: The Personal City Operating System

## The Short Version

inMyCity helps people decide what to do, where to go, and what to know in their city.

It is not another listings app. It is a local-first city companion that brings together curated places, live local context, practical transit, community signal, and an AI city concierge so users can move from curiosity to confident action.

The product promise is simple:

> Open the app and know what to do next.

## Why This Should Exist

Modern city life is fragmented.

Someone trying to plan a simple evening might search restaurants in Google Maps, check Instagram or TikTok for what looks good, browse event platforms for something happening tonight, look up transit separately, ask friends for neighborhood advice, and then use a generic AI assistant to make sense of it all. The user is not short on information. They are short on confidence.

That is the opportunity for inMyCity.

The app should become the trusted layer between inspiration, local truth, and personal action. It should help a user answer real questions:

- What should I do now?
- What is happening around me?
- How do I get there?
- What should I know before I go?
- What is actually worth my time?

The current product already points in this direction. Explore gives users fast access to city categories. Pulse introduces live local awareness and social proof. City Guide helps visitors and newcomers understand the city. CityPilot hints at the most important long-term shift: from browsing a city to asking the city.

## Market Context

The market is moving toward conversational, social, and action-oriented discovery.

[Google Maps Ask Maps and Immersive Navigation](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/) shows that local search is becoming conversational. Google frames Maps as something users can ask complex, real-world questions, not only search by category. That validates the direction of CityPilot: users increasingly expect local products to understand intent, constraints, and context.

[Google Maps Gemini navigation features](https://blog.google/products/maps/gemini-navigation-features-landmark-lens/) also show that AI is moving into the full city journey: finding places along a route, understanding place details, reporting disruptions, and helping users know before they go. This matters because inMyCity should not treat discovery, transit, and local context as separate products. They are one decision loop.

[TikTok GO](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-go?lang=en) shows that social discovery is becoming local commerce. People already discover restaurants, hotels, attractions, and tours through creators and location pages. TikTok is connecting that inspiration to booking. inMyCity should learn from this shift without becoming a pure social feed: community and creator signal should make local decisions more trustworthy and actionable.

[Grounding with Google Maps in the Gemini API](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/grounding-google-maps-gemini-api/) points to the next developer platform pattern: location-aware AI grounded in real-world data. For inMyCity, this means CityPilot must eventually reason over app data: places, events, deals, transit, neighborhoods, itineraries, user saves, time, location, and city context.

[Skyscanner's future travel trends](https://www.skyscanner.net/press-releases/skyscanner-reveals-future-airports-flying-0) reinforce the broader travel shift: AI, social inspiration, and curated experiences are shaping how people plan. The opportunity is not just better search. It is better decisions.

## Product Vision

inMyCity should become the personal city operating system for locals, visitors, and newcomers.

The app should be useful whether someone has lived in Mumbai for years, just landed in San Francisco, moved to Bangalore last month, or is visiting New York City for a weekend. The shared need is the same: make the city easier to understand and act on.

The primary wedge is:

> Locals and visitors who want actionable, city-specific recommendations without juggling maps, search, social media, event sites, and generic AI chat.

The product should win by being local, contextual, trusted, and action-oriented. It should feel less like a database and more like a knowledgeable friend who understands the city, knows what is happening, and can help the user choose.

## Product Pillars

### Explore

Explore is the fast path into the city.

It should help users discover things to do, eat, watch, book, visit, and save. Its job is not to show every possible option. Its job is to organize the city into useful choices.

Explore should prioritize:

- curated categories over exhaustive directories
- mood and intent over raw listings
- useful filters like time, budget, distance, weather, and transit access
- clear next actions: view, save, route, share, plan, ask CityPilot

### Pulse

Pulse is the city's situational awareness layer.

It should answer, "What is happening right now, and what should I pay attention to?" That includes alerts, events, local updates, creator posts, trusted publisher updates, community discussion, and Q&A.

Pulse should become trustworthy before it becomes fully social. The key is not volume. The key is signal.

Pulse should prioritize:

- freshness and source labeling
- trusted local updates
- city-specific context
- community questions that solve real local problems
- creator and business posts only when they help users act

### City Guide

City Guide is the best way to understand a city quickly.

It should serve visitors, newcomers, and locals exploring unfamiliar neighborhoods. This is one of the strongest current surfaces in the app because it gives the city shape: neighborhoods, transit, highlights, itineraries, food, hotels, events, and practical orientation.

City Guide should become the product's evergreen knowledge layer.

It should prioritize:

- neighborhood intelligence
- visitor-ready transit and getting-around guidance
- must-see highlights with local context
- itineraries by trip length, mood, group type, and budget
- practical details that reduce anxiety before arrival

### CityPilot

CityPilot is the conversational layer that turns intent into plans.

It should not be a generic chatbot attached to the app. It should be the app's intelligence interface. Users should be able to ask in natural language and get answers grounded in the same city data that powers Explore, Pulse, and City Guide.

CityPilot should eventually handle questions like:

- "I have three hours in Bandra. What should I do?"
- "Plan a rain-safe family afternoon in Mumbai."
- "What's happening near me tonight under Rs 1500?"
- "Suggest dinner and a movie reachable by metro."
- "I'm visiting San Francisco for two days. What should I not miss?"

CityPilot should become central, but browsing should remain first-class. Some users want to ask; some want to scan. The best product supports both.

## Core User Journeys

### I'm Free Today

The user has time and wants a recommendation.

inMyCity should combine mood, time available, city, location, budget, weather, distance, opening hours, and transit access to recommend plans. This is the highest-frequency local use case and should become a primary habit loop.

Success looks like the user saying: "That sounds good. Let's go."

### I'm Visiting

The user needs to understand the city quickly.

inMyCity should explain neighborhoods, transit, highlights, food, safety/context, hotels, events, and itineraries. The app should reduce planning stress and help visitors make better use of limited time.

Success looks like the user saying: "I know where to start."

### I Live Here

The user wants local awareness and better everyday decisions.

inMyCity should surface what changed, what is happening, what is nearby, and what is worth doing. The app should make the user's own city feel more legible and alive.

Success looks like the user saying: "I would not have known that otherwise."

## Experience Principles

### Actionable Over Exhaustive

The app should not try to show everything. It should help users choose. Ranking, grouping, summaries, and recommendations matter more than raw quantity.

### Local Truth Over Generic Content

Every city should feel specific. Mumbai should not feel like New York with different labels. Neighborhoods, transit, food, weather, culture, and local constraints should shape the experience.

### Intent-First Over Category-First

Categories are useful, but user intent is stronger. "Date night," "rain-safe," "family day," "under Rs 1000," and "near a metro station" are more natural than forcing users to start with a taxonomy.

### Trustworthy Signal Over Noisy Feeds

Pulse should earn trust through source labeling, freshness, relevance, and moderation. A smaller feed with high confidence is better than a larger feed that feels random.

### AI As A Guide, Not A Gimmick

CityPilot should not exist to show that the app has AI. It should reduce effort, connect dots across the app, and help users make decisions they could not easily make from a static list.

## Strategic Priorities

### 1. Make CityPilot Understand App Data

CityPilot should be grounded in places, events, deals, transit, neighborhoods, itineraries, and city guide content. This is the difference between a generic assistant and a true city companion.

The product should move toward answers that can cite or reference app entities, recommend next actions, and create plans from real app inventory.

### 2. Make Recommendations Contextual

Recommendations should use city, time, weather, distance, budget, mood, trip length, group type, and transit access. The more context the app understands, the more it can move from search results to useful decisions.

### 3. Make Pulse Trustworthy Before Fully Social

Pulse should start as a curated local awareness system. Community and creator content should be added in ways that improve confidence, not noise.

Source labels, freshness, and clear categories are critical.

### 4. Make City Guide The Best Newcomer Experience

City Guide should become the app's most polished knowledge surface. It is where the app can feel deeply local, especially for visitors and people who have recently moved.

It should answer the questions people ask before they feel comfortable in a city.

### 5. Reduce Category Sprawl Through Intent

The app has many useful categories. The next product step is not simply adding more. It is organizing them around user intent so the experience feels intelligent rather than broad.

## North Star

The North Star metric should be:

> Successful city decisions per user per week.

A successful city decision is a moment where inMyCity helps a user choose and act:

- save a place
- open a route
- start an itinerary
- share a plan
- ask CityPilot and follow a recommendation
- view details for a recommended place or event
- return to a saved or planned item

This metric keeps the team focused on outcomes, not content volume.

## Near-Term Roadmap Themes

### Data-Grounded CityPilot

Connect CityPilot to the app's own city data so answers can recommend actual places, events, deals, routes, and itineraries.

### Personalized Plan Generation

Let users generate plans by city, mood, time, budget, weather, distance, and group type. Plans should be editable, saveable, and shareable.

### Save And Share Itinerary Flows

Make it easy to move from discovery to a lightweight plan. Saving should not be a dead end; it should become the start of a future decision.

### Stronger Search And Filters

Search should support intent, not only keywords. Users should be able to find "quiet cafes to work from," "events tonight," "rain-safe family activities," and "restaurants near transit."

### Trust, Freshness, And Source Labeling

Pulse and local updates should clearly show where information came from, when it was updated, and why it is relevant.

## What This Means For The Product Team

The product team should prioritize depth of decision support over breadth of categories.

Every roadmap item should be judged by one question:

> Does this help a user make a better city decision?

That means:

- CityPilot should become a core product surface, not an accessory.
- Explore should evolve from categories into contextual recommendations.
- Pulse should focus on trusted signal before social scale.
- City Guide should be polished as the evergreen city knowledge layer.
- Data quality, freshness, and entity relationships are strategic product assets.

The product should feel like it knows the city and knows what the user is trying to do. That is the difference between an app people browse once and a companion they return to every week.

## Final Vision

inMyCity should become the app people open when they want to understand or act in a city.

For locals, it makes the city feel more alive and manageable.

For visitors, it makes the city feel easier to enter.

For newcomers, it makes the city feel less intimidating.

For the product team, the direction is clear:

Build the trusted city companion that turns local information into confident action.
